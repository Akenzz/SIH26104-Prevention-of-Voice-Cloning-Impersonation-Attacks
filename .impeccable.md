## Design Context

### Users
Hackathon judges are the primary audience during a short, high-stakes demonstration. The same app must also make sense to a fraud-review or contact-centre operations user who needs to assess whether live or uploaded speech warrants additional verification. Their job is to understand the risk quickly, see why it was assigned, and decide whether to escalate—not to treat the result as proof of a speaker's identity.

### Brand Personality
Assured, intelligible, and technically credible. The product should feel calm enough for an executive reviewer, but provide the evidence and system health detail a technical evaluator expects. It must never overstate model certainty or imply that it performs identity verification.

### Aesthetic Direction
An editorial trust cockpit: warm near-black and parchment neutrals, precise red/amber/green risk cues, restrained signal-line motifs, and a strong typographic hierarchy. The home view is presentation-ready and low-cognitive-load; detailed signal, model, and timing evidence is disclosed in clearly labelled review views. Avoid generic neon "cybersecurity" styling and gratuitous glass effects. The Flutter application must work well on a judge's tablet as well as a desktop demo display, with risk communicated by text and icon as well as colour.

### Design Principles
1. Make the current decision and next action obvious within a glance.
2. Pair every risk value with human-readable evidence and an explicit limitation.
3. Reveal technical depth progressively instead of overwhelming a first-time judge.
4. Treat unavailable or poor-quality audio as unavailable—not as safe.
5. Use motion only to explain a changing live state and respect reduced-motion preferences.
